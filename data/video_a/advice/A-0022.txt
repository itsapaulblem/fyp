CASE: A-0022 — Goalkeeper considerations when defending corners, clip 1 (Norway, Einar Fauskanger)

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Norway goalkeeper (Einar Fauskanger), defending an inswinging corner
while man-marked by an attacking player in his immediate vicinity.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Develop a goalkeeper's
ability to maintain visual and spatial control when man-marked at a
corner — reading the delivery type to set the correct starting
position, holding patience before committing, and timing a movement to
create separation from a marker before attacking the ball.

Evidence (per FIFA's senior football and goalkeeping expert Pascal
Zuberbühler, discussing this clip): This clip shows Norway goalkeeper
Einar Fauskanger being marked by an attacking player standing in his
immediate vicinity. Before the ball is kicked, Fauskanger is
positioned close to his goal line (consistent with defending an
inswinger), holds an open body shape, and has a good view of the ball.
As the inswinger is delivered, he patiently times his response before
manoeuvring around the man-marker to create space, then attacks and
punches the ball clear. This is presented as an example of the
constant goalkeeper responsibilities the article describes: having a
clear view of the ball, judging its height, showing patience to read
the trajectory before moving, maintaining an open body shape, setting
a starting position based on whether the delivery is an inswinger or
outswinger, and ensuring space to claim/interact with the ball near
the goal area.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Per Pascal Zuberbühler's stated principles in the article (general
guidance applicable to this clip): train goalkeepers to set their
starting position based on whether the delivery is an inswinger
(closer to the goal line) or an outswinger (slightly further out),
always maintaining an open body shape to preserve a view of the ball
and surrounding space. Add a mannequin or live attacker standing in
the goalkeeper's immediate vicinity to simulate being man-marked, and
coach the goalkeeper to judge why the attacker is positioned in front
of or behind them (as this can indicate the intended ball trajectory),
while working to ensure the attacker's positioning does not impede
their movement. Rehearse the specific technique of manoeuvring/creating
space away from the marker with correct timing (not moving too early)
before attacking crosses of varying delivery types and heights.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Goalkeeper's starting position correctly reflects the type of
  delivery expected (closer to goal line for inswingers, slightly
  further out for outswingers).
- Goalkeeper maintains an open body shape, preserving visual contact
  with both the ball and the attacker marking them.
- Goalkeeper does not commit to movement early, showing patience to
  read the ball's trajectory first.
- Goalkeeper successfully creates separation from the marking attacker
  through well-timed movement.
- Goalkeeper is able to attack and claim/punch the ball clear despite
  the attacker's proximity.
