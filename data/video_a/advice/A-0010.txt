CASE: A-0010 — Güler contribution, clip 1

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Real Madrid attacking midfielder (Arda Güler), operating in central
and inside-channel space against Juventus.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Develop the ability to
retain possession under pressure in tight central spaces using small
touches and disguised body language, delaying opposition pressing
decisions long enough to create a decisive pass or 1v1 opportunity for
a teammate.

Evidence (per UEFA Technical Observer Jan Peder Jalland, discussing
this clip): Güler keeps the ball close to his feet while operating in
limited space, using small touches that allow him to twist and turn
under pressure. He avoids giving away clear signals about his
intentions, so opponents cannot anticipate when or where the pass will
go — this uncertainty makes opposing players hesitant to press him
aggressively, preserving space around him. Jalland's direct
assessment: "He takes small touches and can twist and turn and produce
quality in such small spaces. Also, he doesn't give away any clear
signals so it seems the pass could come at any time. This means that
players are a bit afraid to press him and give away space." The
sequence culminates in Güler setting up a 1v1 opportunity for
Vinícius Júnior in the buildup to Bellingham's goal. Wider match
context notes Güler ranked second among Real Madrid players for both
key passes (six) and passes into the final third (nine), and was the
second-most frequent Madrid player (after Bellingham) to receive the
ball between the lines (12 times) — though these are match-wide
figures rather than specific to this clip.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Per Jalland's general coaching reflections in the article (not written
specifically for this clip): begin with mannequins and passes played
between the lines, with a player moving to find a striker or winger in
behind, then progress to combination play and finishing. Add central
defenders as mannequins for midfielders, then gradually introduce live
opposition, increasing difficulty by adjusting pitch size and player
numbers — smaller pitches with fewer players increase the demand for
close control, awareness, and quick decision-making under less time
and space, while larger setups with more players are closer to
matchplay but yield fewer repetitions.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Player keeps the ball close and under control while under pressure
  in tight space.
- Player disguises intent (body shape, eyes, timing) so opponents
  cannot predict the pass.
- Opposing defenders are visibly hesitant to press aggressively,
  giving the player extra time on the ball.
- Player successfully releases a pass that creates a clear 1v1 or
  attacking opportunity for a teammate.

REVIEWER PROVENANCE
The coaching commentary above is directly attributed to Jan Peder Jalland
(UEFA Technical Observer; coach of Norway's Under-21 men's national
team), a named, credentialed source quoted in the article discussing
this specific clip
