CASE: A-0024 — Goalkeeper considerations when defending corners, clip 3 (Morocco, Yanis Benchaouch)

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Morocco goalkeeper (Yanis Benchaouch), defending a corner within a
hybrid marking system against Korea Republic.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Develop a goalkeeper's
ability to remain focused solely on the ball amid deliberate opposition
movement designed to create confusion, trusting zonal cover in front
of goal, and to time a jump correctly to maximise reach/height on the
intervention.

Evidence (per Pascal Zuberbühler, FIFA's senior football and
goalkeeping expert, discussing this clip): This clip shows chaotic
movements by Korea Republic's players designed to disorganise
Morocco's defence and lose their markers. Morocco's goalkeeper
Benchaouch is under no direct pressure and has a good view, while the
front of his goal area is protected by zonal players (Morocco using a
hybrid marking system, combining player marking and zonal marking).
Benchaouch is aware of the chaos around him but is not distracted by
it, staying focused on the ball. He waits before timing his jump to
generate great height, tipping the ball away. The article notes that,
tournament-wide, first contacts by goalkeepers in hybrid marking
systems decreased by 4% compared to the FIFA U-20 World Cup Argentina
2023.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Per the article's general framing of hybrid marking systems (not
written specifically for this clip): rehearse corner defending in a
hybrid setup (player marking combined with zonal players at the front
of the goal area), deliberately introducing chaotic, unpredictable
attacking movement patterns designed to disrupt marking assignments.
Coach the goalkeeper specifically to filter out this movement and keep
visual focus locked on the ball and its trajectory, rather than
reacting to nearby player movement. Add repetition on jump timing —
waiting rather than committing early — to build the habit of
generating maximum height only at the correct moment.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Goalkeeper maintains visual focus on the ball throughout, despite
  distracting or chaotic attacking movement nearby.
- Goalkeeper does not react prematurely to opposition players'
  movement patterns.
- Zonal players in front of the goal area provide the intended
  protection, reducing direct pressure on the goalkeeper.
- Goalkeeper's jump is well-timed, generating maximum height for the
  intervention.
- Goalkeeper successfully intervenes (tips/punches/catches) the
  delivered ball.
