CASE: A-0014 — Champions League tactical insights: Chelsea counterpressing — Cucurella clip 1

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Chelsea left-back (Marc Cucurella)

COACHING INTERVENTION
Develop the balance and patience to hold a low defensive stance without committing early in a 1v1, engaging only at the optimal moment to win the ball.

VISIBLE EVIDENCE / HUMAN OBSERVATION
This clip is the first in a five-clip UEFA video series on Cucurella's 1v1 defending against Barcelona's Lamine Yamal, in a match UEFA's game insights unit credits as central to Chelsea's biggest-ever win over Barcelona (3-0) and Cucurella's Player of the Match award. Per the article, clip one 'showcases his low centre of gravity and his patience as he does not commit himself early but waits for the right moment before snapping into the challenge.' No single named individual is quoted specifically on this clip (Rui Faria is quoted on clips three and four; Roberto Martínez on clip five), but the article notes UEFA's technical observers found much to admire in Cucurella's defending across all pitch zones, from a low block (clip two) to higher up the pitch.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Per Rui Faria's (UEFA Technical Observer) general coaching drill described in the article, used as a representative intervention for this example: use a small-sided pitch to create 1v1, 2v2, or 3v3 situations, with the coach calling the numbers each time. Add two or three 'neutral players' on the side of the pitch to create numerical variations (e.g. 2v1, 3v2). The attacking side (with any numerical advantage) aims to finish quickly; the defending side practices the balance between patience (when defending with fewer numbers) and pressing (when numbers are equal). Faria's stated core principles for 1v1 defending: body positioning and balance; focus on ball and space; and identifying the opponent's weaker side.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Defender maintains a consistently low centre of gravity and balanced stance throughout the approach.
- Defender does not lunge or commit to contact prematurely.
- Defender closes the gap only as the right moment to challenge arises.
- Challenge, when made, is timed to win the ball rather than simply react to the attacker's movement.
