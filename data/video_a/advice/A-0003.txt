CASE: A-0003 — Argentina attacking corners: freeing the edge — clip 3 vs Poland

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Argentina attacking unit

COACHING INTERVENTION
General objective drawn from the article's stated strategies for this routine: commit numbers to the box in a tiered formation to occupy markers, use short combination play to draw the edge-of-box defender away, and free a technically able passer/shooter (Fernández) to strike from the edge of the area.

VISIBLE EVIDENCE / HUMAN OBSERVATION
The article does not describe specific player positions or decoy movements for this clip. It states only that this is a further example of the same corner routine seen in clips 1 and 2, this time resulting in an off-target attempt against Poland. The general strategies behind the routine, per the article, are: committing 5-6 players to attack in tiered formation, engaging player-for-player markers with multiple attackers, overloading the front post or winning a flick-on there, using a third man for short corners, and targeting the front post or centre of goal. The 'special strategy' behind this specific Argentina routine is playing short to draw defenders out, linking up around the box to free space for Fernández on the edge, and using the technical ability of Messi and Di María to work the ball to the front post or set up a shot.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Representative general practice for the coaching intervention illustrated by this example: rehearse corner routines where 5-6 attackers commit to the box in staggered depth, with designated players tasked with engaging markers 1-for-1 and a 'third man' used to work the short corner. Emphasise combination play (give-and-go / third-man passing) around the edge of the box specifically to create separation for a nominated shooter, and coach the front-post overload or flick-on as an alternate release if the edge option isn't on.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Attacking group commits enough numbers (5-6) in tiered depth to occupy defensive markers throughout the move.
- Short-corner combination play successfully draws at least one defender out of the edge-of-box zone.
- Designated shooter receives on the edge of the area with time to strike (even if, as in this instance, the attempt goes off target).
- Front-post overload or flick-on option is available as an alternate route when the edge-of-box release isn't open.
