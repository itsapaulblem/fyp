CASE: A-0023 — Goalkeeper considerations when defending corners, clip 2 (Panama, Cecilio Burgess)

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Panama goalkeeper (Cecilio Burgess), defending a corner within a 5v5
player-marking system against Korea Republic.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Enable a goalkeeper in a
player-marking defensive corner system to trust a designated defender
to occupy a nearby attacker, so the goalkeeper can focus solely on the
ball and time a late movement to create space to attack and claim it.

Evidence (per Pascal Zuberbühler, FIFA's senior football and
goalkeeping expert, discussing this clip): This clip shows Panama
matching Korea Republic in a 5v5 player-marking system, with one zonal
player protecting the front of the goal area. A defender marks the
attacker positioned near the goalkeeper, ensuring Burgess is free to
manage the space and see the ball. Zuberbühler notes this matters
because the goalkeeper can see the full scenario, knows all attackers
are accounted for, and can focus on the ball. Just before the corner
is kicked, the goalkeeper is penned in, but he is patient and makes a
crucial movement with great timing, creating space so he can attack
the ball, while his defender takes care of the attacker. The article
separately notes that, up to and including the round of 16 at this
tournament, first contacts made by goalkeepers using a player-marking
system increased by 7% compared to the FIFA U-20 World Cup Argentina
2023.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Per the article's general framing of player-marking systems (not
written specifically for this clip): rehearse corner defending with a
full player-marking setup (e.g. 5v5 plus a zonal player at the front
of the goal area), explicitly assigning one defender to occupy any
attacker stationed in the goalkeeper's immediate vicinity. Coach the
goalkeeper to communicate and confirm all attackers are accounted for
before the delivery, then to hold a disciplined starting position
(even if initially restricted/penned in by the marker) and practice a
late, precisely timed movement to create separation and attack the
ball, rather than moving early and losing sight of the play.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- A designated defender is clearly assigned to occupy the attacker
  nearest the goalkeeper, rather than leaving that responsibility
  ambiguous.
- Goalkeeper communicates to confirm all attackers are accounted for
  before the ball is delivered.
- Goalkeeper remains patient and does not move prematurely, even while
  initially restricted by the nearby attacker's positioning.
- Goalkeeper's eventual movement is precisely timed, creating just
  enough space to attack the ball.
- Goalkeeper successfully claims/wins the first contact on the
  delivered ball.
