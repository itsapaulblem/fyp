CASE: A-0012 — Arsenal's right-sided overload, clip 1

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Arsenal attacking unit, right-sided overload against Sporting CP's
5-2-3 defensive shape.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Use a central
midfielder's movement into a wide channel to deliberately draw a
covering centre-back out of the defensive line, creating space for a
winger to attack in behind and deliver a cross for a teammate to
finish.

Evidence (per UEFA's technical analysis unit): Arsenal's game plan
against Sporting CP was to force Sporting into a flat back five and
exploit space either side of Sporting's midfield two (Morita and
Hjulmand) within their 5-2-3 defensive shape, with the right flank in
particular targeted for overloads and combinations — the source of
Arsenal's two early goals. In this clip, Arsenal's attack builds down
the right with a structured push toward the defensive line involving
five players. Declan Rice moves into the right channel, drawing out
Sporting's left-sided centre-back, Gonçalo Inácio. Rice's movement
opens space for Saka, who capitalises with a well-timed run and
delivers a cross that Kai Havertz converts.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Positional/rondo-to-goal practice against a back-five or back-four
shape: attacking side commits players (structured five-player push, as
in this clip) toward one side of the pitch. A central midfielder is
coached to time a run into the wide channel specifically to
occupy/drag a covering centre-back, rather than to receive the ball
directly. As the centre-back engages, the wide attacker (winger) times
a run into the space just vacated, receives, and delivers a cross for
an onrushing striker to finish. Progress from unopposed shape work to
opposed practice with a defined defensive block (e.g. 5-2-3) to
replicate the real spacing being exploited.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Central midfielder's run into the channel is deliberate and draws
  the covering centre-back out of position, rather than being
  incidental.
- The vacated space is recognised and attacked promptly by the winger.
- Winger's run is well-timed to arrive in space rather than into a
  marked position.
- Cross is delivered with enough quality for the target striker to
  convert.
- The wider structural push (numbers committed to that side) is
  maintained throughout the sequence, not abandoned once the ball
  starts moving.
