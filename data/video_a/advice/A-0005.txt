CASE: A-0005 — Cameroon corner front-post overload, clip 1

COACHING INTERVENTION
Objective: Establish numerical superiority in the front-post zone
against a zonal defensive setup by committing four runners there in a
tiered structure, then convert via first contact or a flick-on across
goal to a back-post runner.

Rationale (from source text): Cameroon's general approach to attacking
corners focused on delivering the ball straight into the penalty area,
with this clip as the first example. The routine placed heavy emphasis
on overloading the area around the front post, designed to counter an
opposition using a zonal defensive setup. Four players concentrated
around the six-yard box, all making runs towards the front post to
establish numerical superiority in that zone. Behind them, a second
wave of attackers included one player running to the back post and two
more tasked with covering the edge of the box. For the corner to be
effective, Cameroon needed either to score directly from the first
contact, or to flick the ball across goal to the back-post runner —
requiring both the quality of the delivery and the timing of the runs
to be precise. The article's stated first step of the routine is
sending four runners into the area where the opposition has the fewest
defenders, establishing numerical superiority there before the
delivery arrives; if the ball beats the first defender into that zone,
the deeper zonal defender is powerless to deal with the cross, and no
other defender can recover across in time.

REPRESENTATIVE PRACTICE
Small-sided corner rehearsal against a zonal defensive shape: attacking
side sends four players to occupy and overload the front-post zone,
with a second wave of three players positioned deeper — one targeting
the back post, two covering the edge of the box. Coach for precise
timing of the front-post runners' movement relative to the delivery,
and rehearse both finishing options (direct contact at the front post,
and flick-on to the back-post runner) so players can react to whichever
presents itself based on the delivery.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Four attackers successfully occupy and outnumber defenders in the
  front-post zone before the ball is delivered.
- Delivery beats the first defender and reaches the overloaded zone.
- Zonal defender behind the contested zone is unable to intervene on
  the ball.
- No other defender is able to recover across in time to affect the
  situation at the front post.
- Front-post group either scores from first contact or successfully
  flicks the ball on to the back-post runner.
