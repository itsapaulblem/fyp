CASE: A-0013 — Arsenal's right-sided overload, clip 2

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Arsenal attacking unit, right-sided 5v4 overload building to the
first goal against Sporting CP.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Combine a numerical
overload on one side of the pitch with disciplined off-ball
positioning, a precisely weighted one-touch setup pass, and
synchronised timed runs (one in behind, one across the box) to
convert a created overload into a goal.

Evidence (per UEFA's technical analysis unit): This clip shows the
build-up to Arsenal's first goal, starting with a 5v4 overload Arsenal
create on the right. Declan Rice creates space by deliberately
avoiding movement toward the ball, instead positioning himself to
exploit gaps. Martin Ødegaard then plays a precise pass to Rice's
right foot, set up so Rice needs only one touch before delivering a
through ball, timed perfectly for Jurriën Timber's run in behind.
Gabriel Martinelli cuts across from the back post to attack the
five-metre box and score the goal.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Overload-to-finish practice (e.g. 5v4 in a wide channel progressing to
goal): one player is coached specifically to hold a position away from
the ball, reading gaps rather than checking to receive, to stay
available for a one-touch lay-off. A supplying player rehearses
playing accurately weighted passes to the receiving player's stronger
foot so a first-time through ball is possible without a controlling
touch. A third player times a run in behind off that through ball,
while a fourth player rehearses a supporting run across the face of
goal (back post to five-metre box) to arrive for the finish. Progress
from unopposed shape work to a defending back four to replicate the
numerical overload being exploited.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Attacking side establishes a genuine numerical overload (more
  attackers than defenders) in the working area before the key pass.
- The link player holds position intelligently rather than checking to
  the ball, staying available for a one-touch pass.
- Setup pass is weighted and directed to the receiving player's
  stronger foot, enabling an immediate one-touch release.
- The through ball is timed precisely for the runner's break in
  behind, neither too early nor too late.
- A supporting player arrives across the box (e.g. back post to
  five-metre area) in time to convert the resulting chance.
