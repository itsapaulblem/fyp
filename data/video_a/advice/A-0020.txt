CASE: A-0020 — Cucurella excels, clip 1 (Chelsea counterpressing)

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Chelsea team, counterpressing to regain possession in the middle and
attacking third against Barcelona.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Develop the immediate,
aggressive reaction needed to win the ball back quickly after losing
it, and the decision-making to progress directly and quickly once
possession is regained, rather than resetting into a slow buildup.

Evidence (per the source article): This is the opening clip of the
first video in the article, illustrating Chelsea's collective
intensity via counterpressing during their 3-0 win over Barcelona.
This was a team who, on losing the ball, worked aggressively to win it
back, as seen with Malo Gusto's challenge to regain the ball — and once
they had it back, they were quick and direct in progressing it
forward. The article notes this intensity is a recurring theme for
Chelsea under manager Enzo Maresca, also referencing a similar pattern
in a prior UEFA Conference League final analysis against Real Betis.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Small-sided possession-to-transition practice: on a coached or random
turnover, the team without the ball must press immediately and
aggressively to win it back (rather than retreat), with a specific
emphasis on committing to challenges (as Gusto does in this clip)
rather than delaying or standing off. Once the ball is regained,
reward/require quick, direct forward passes or dribbles rather than
safe sideways/backward passes, reinforcing the "regain and go"
pattern. Time-pressure constraints (e.g. must play forward within 1-2
touches of winning the ball) can help ingrain the directness.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Players closest to the ball react immediately and aggressively upon
  losing possession.
- At least one player commits fully to a challenge to win the ball
  back, rather than jockeying passively.
- The ball is regained through that committed challenge.
- Immediately after the regain, the team progresses the ball quickly
  and directly upfield rather than resetting slowly.
