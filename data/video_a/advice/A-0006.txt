CASE: A-0006 — Cameroon corner near-post overload vs Switzerland, clip 2

COACHING INTERVENTION
Objective: Use a two-player short-corner setup to draw defenders out of
position, while still committing runners to overload the front post
and cover the back post/penalty-spot zones — and ensure delivery
quality matches the movement so the overload can actually be exploited.

Rationale (from source text): Cameroon moved two players out of the
penalty area to take the corner, with the stated aim of drawing the
Swiss defenders out of position. Compared to clip 1's four front-post
runners, this time Cameroon sent three runners towards the front post,
with two further players making runs to the back post and the penalty
spot respectively. The out-swinging delivery, however, was off target
on this occasion, and the ball was cleared by the second zonal
defender.

REPRESENTATIVE PRACTICE
Small-sided corner rehearsal where two attackers start outside the box
specifically to draw defenders out before the corner is taken (rather
than delivering directly from a single corner-taker). Three attackers
time runs to the front post, one targets the back post, and one
targets the penalty spot. Dedicate reps specifically to out-swinging
delivery technique and consistency, since this clip's breakdown was a
delivery/execution failure rather than a structural one — emphasise it
must consistently beat the first defender and reach the front-post
runners rather than drift off target.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Two players positioned outside the box visibly draw at least one
  defender out of the central defensive shape before the corner is
  taken.
- Three front-post runners arrive with correct timing relative to the
  delivery.
- Back-post and penalty-spot runners hold their zones to offer a
  second/third option.
- Delivery is out-swinging but on target, reaching the front-post zone
  rather than being cut out by the second zonal defender.
- If delivery is off, review specifically whether it was a
  technical/execution issue (as in this instance) versus a
  structural/movement issue.
