CASE: A-0007 — PSG wide free kick vs Inter Miami: blocking the last defender

COACHING INTERVENTION
Objective: Exploit a zonal defensive line's structural weaknesses at
the far post — numerical inferiority, closed body orientation, an
undersized last defender, and predictable early line movement — by
synchronising decoy/offside-holding runners with a timed, legal
screening block on the last defender to free a runner from depth for
an unmarked header at the far post.

Rationale (from source text): This example comes from the FIFA Club
World Cup 2025 game between PSG and Inter Miami. Inter Miami's
defensive setup exposed the last defender in their zonal line: five
players were committed to the ball/wall area, leaving only five in the
zonal line itself, creating a 4v2 numerical disadvantage at the far
post. The line held close to the edge of the box (~13m out), leaving
space in behind. Defenders adopted closed body orientations, limiting
visual contact with attackers behind them, and the last defender was
undersized relative to the block he'd face. The line stepped up early,
timed to the taker's run-up, which reduced attackers' positional edge
but also let offside-starting attackers regain onside position as the
kick was delivered.

PSG designed their routine around these specific vulnerabilities: two
attackers made runs from depth while two more held offside positions
to occupy the last defenders and disguise the real threat. All
movement was triggered off Vitinha's first step. Marquinhos' run
pulled Falcon (the last defender) infield, further weakening far-post
cover, while Neves timed his run to stay onside. Pacho, reacting to the
same trigger, regained an onside position as the line stepped up
early, then applied the block. PSG deliberately used Pacho — their
more physically dominant centre-back — against Inter Miami's smaller
last defender, screening with his back to the defender (avoiding arm
use or frontal contact) to block the recovery run without conceding a
foul, while staying available for any second ball. The inswinging
delivery met Neves' well-timed run from deep at the far post, arriving
unmarked for a free header, while Falcon — anticipating the ball would
go out of play and unaware of the run behind him — raised his arms
rather than defending the space.

REPRESENTATIVE PRACTICE
Set-piece rehearsal against a zonal defensive shape mimicking the
identified vulnerabilities: attacking side commits two runners from
depth and two players holding offside positions to occupy/confuse the
last defenders. All movement is triggered off a single, agreed cue
(e.g. the taker's first step of the run-up). Rehearse the blocking
player (matched physically against the opposition's weakest last
defender) using a back-to-defender screen — no frontal contact, no arm
use — to legally block the recovery run while remaining available for
a second ball. Simultaneously rehearse the drag run that pulls the
last defender infield, and the depth runner's timed, onside run to the
far post. Finish with crosses/deliveries specifically inswinging to
meet the far-post run.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Attacking side identifies and exploits a genuine numerical advantage
  at the far post before the kick is taken.
- Decoy/offside-holding attackers successfully occupy or confuse the
  last defenders without being the actual target.
- All attacking movement triggers off the same cue, arriving
  synchronised rather than piecemeal.
- The drag-run attacker pulls the last defender out of position,
  reducing far-post cover.
- The blocking player is matched against a physically weaker last
  defender and executes a legal screen (back-to-defender, no arms, no
  frontal contact) that stops the recovery run without conceding a
  foul.
- The runner from depth times his run to remain onside and arrives at
  the far post unmarked.
- Delivery is inswinging and precisely meets the runner's arrival for
  a clean header on goal.
