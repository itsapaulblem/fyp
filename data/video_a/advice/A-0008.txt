CASE: A-0008 — Botafogo defending a wide free kick: avoiding the last-defender block

COACHING INTERVENTION
Objective: Enable the last defender in a zonal free-kick line to
individually resist blocking actions through open body orientation and
a wider starting position, preserving blindside awareness and
manoeuvring room without needing to alter the team's overall defensive
shape or line height.

Rationale (from source text): This clip is presented as one of four
defensive counter-strategies to the last-defender blocking tactic
described elsewhere in the article (see A-0007, PSG vs Inter Miami).
The article frames it as a subtler, more proactive adjustment compared
to simply shifting the whole line towards the far post, which can
leave the front-post zone exposed instead. The Botafogo clip
specifically highlights two adjustments from the last defender: an
open body orientation, which helps him maintain visual contact with
the attacker on his blindside, and starting a few steps wider from the
rest of the line, which gives him more room to negotiate/avoid the
block when it comes. The article's broader framing across all four
defensive solutions is that they share a common aim — to disrupt
blocking actions before they can be executed — reinforcing the
importance of proactive rather than passive or static defending.

REPRESENTATIVE PRACTICE
Individual/unit defending practice for the last defender in a zonal
wide-free-kick line: rehearse holding an open body shape (rather than
square-on/closed to the play) so the defender can track both the ball
and any blindside attacker simultaneously. Pair this with a starting
position a few steps wider than the rest of the zonal line, giving
room to step around or resist an incoming screen/block. Run repeated
reps against an attacker attempting a blocking run so the defender
practises recognising the block early (due to better visual contact)
and adjusting his body/footwork to get past it, rather than being
caught passively.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Last defender maintains visual contact with the blindside attacker
  throughout the delivery, not just the ball.
- Defender's starting position is visibly wider than the rest of the
  zonal line, without compromising overall line coverage.
- Defender recognises an incoming block early enough to adjust his
  body position or footwork.
- Defender is able to negotiate around or resist the block rather than
  being pinned/screened out of the play.
- The far-post zone remains covered without requiring the whole line
  to shift or drop, preserving front-post coverage as well.
