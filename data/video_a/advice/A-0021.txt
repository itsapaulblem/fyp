CASE: A-0021 — Chelsea collective intensity, clip 3

TEAM / ROLE BEING COACHED
Chelsea team, sustained pressing and direct attacking transition
against Barcelona.

COACHING INTERVENTION
Concise intervention tied to visible evidence: Sustain collective
pressing intensity long enough to force a turnover in a dangerous
area, then convert that turnover immediately into a fast,
minimal-touch attack that creates a 1v1 opportunity.

Evidence (per the source article): Chelsea and Barcelona's intensity
and speed came together for Chelsea's second goal, featured in this
clip: Barcelona could not escape Chelsea's pressure, Andrey Santos
intercepted the ball, and four touches later Estêvão was taking on Pau
Cubarsí. This clip is presented as the culmination of two earlier clips
in the same video — clip one showing Chelsea's counterpressing (Malo
Gusto's challenge to regain possession) and clip two showing Chelsea's
focus on getting the ball rapidly behind Barcelona's high line — with
this clip showing intensity and speed combining directly into a goal.

REPRESENTATIVE TRAINING PRACTICE
Pressing-to-transition practice: set up a possession exercise where
the defending team is instructed to sustain coordinated pressure
without allowing the opposition an easy out-ball, rather than pressing
in short bursts. On winning the ball (via interception, as with Santos
here), immediately apply a touch-limit constraint (e.g. maximum 3-4
touches before reaching a 1v1 or shooting position) to force fast,
direct decision-making. Rotate which player is the designated
interceptor/first attacker to build broad comfort with converting
regains into quick attacking situations.

OBSERVABLE SUCCESS CUES
- Pressing is sustained and coordinated long enough that the
  opposition cannot find a comfortable pass out.
- The turnover is won through an interception rather than a rushed
  clearance or long ball.
- The team in possession after the turnover uses very few touches to
  progress into a dangerous area.
- The sequence results in a clear 1v1 opportunity for an attacker in a
  dangerous area.
- Players show awareness to support the ball-winner immediately,
  enabling quick combination play rather than an isolated run.
