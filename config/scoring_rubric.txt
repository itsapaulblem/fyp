FOOTBALL MLLM HUMAN SCORING RUBRIC
Rubric version: 1.0

1. PURPOSE

Use this single rubric to evaluate whether supplying a human-annotated analogous
Dataset A case changes an MLLM's recognition and coaching advice for the same
Dataset B sequence. Retrieval-assisted performance must not be described as
independent video understanding.

Score recognition and coaching separately. A response can recognise the sequence
correctly but give weak advice, or misrecognise it while giving plausible generic
advice. Report every dimension separately. Do not calculate one overall mark.

Use the private human review of the sampled Dataset B frames as the principal
reference for visible events. SoccerNet's hidden event label may assist recognition
checking after the visual reference is completed, but it is not coaching ground
truth and must never be exposed to the model in an automatic condition.

2. LITERATURE BASIS

The dimensions are informed by these reviewed sources. They motivate the rubric
but do not validate this exact open-ended football scoring procedure.

- S08, Revealing Single Frame Bias for Video-and-Language Learning:
  https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.29/
  Supports checking visible and temporal evidence instead of assuming that a
  multi-frame answer demonstrates temporal understanding.
- S11, Too Many Frames, Not All Useful / LVNet:
  https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.164/
  Supports checking whether the selected frames provide useful event evidence.
- S16, MRAG-Bench:
  https://openreview.net/forum?id=Usklli4gMc
  Supports evaluating retrieved multimodal context separately because noisy
  retrieval can help or harm different models.
- S20, What Makes Good Examples for Visual In-Context Learning?:
  https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/398ae57ed4fda79d0781c65c926d667b-Abstract-Conference.html
  Supports comparing relevant examples with random examples.
- S22, CLIP:
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/radford21a.html
  Supports the pixel-embedding representation used for automatic retrieval.
- S28, SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction:
  https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/html/Somers_SoccerNet_Game_State_Reconstruction_End-to-End_Athlete_Tracking_and_Identification_on_CVPRW_2024_paper.html
  Establishes the Dataset B source context and the limits of its labels as
  coaching references.
- S31, X-VARS:
  https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/html/Held_X-VARS_Introducing_Explainability_in_Football_Refereeing_with_Multi-Modal_Large_Language_CVPRW_2024_paper.html
  Supports separate human assessment of football event recognition and generated
  natural-language explanation.
- S33, ChatGPT-generated running training plans:
  https://doi.org/10.52082/jssm.2024.56
  Supports structured human checking of generated sports advice while remaining
  indirect, text-only evidence.
- S35, POPE:
  https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.20/
  Supports evaluating claims about absent visual content as hallucinations.
- S36, VideoHallucer:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16338
  Informs the object/relation, temporal, semantic-detail, and extrinsic
  hallucination categories used below.

3. REQUIRED SCORING ORDER

Follow this order for every response:

1) Hide the condition name, model identity, Dataset A case, retrieval result, and
   all other condition-revealing information.
2) Read the model's RECOGNITION section and score every recognition dimension.
3) Record whether a critical visible event was missed.
4) Reveal only the information needed to evaluate the supplied case or advice.
5) Score analogy relevance where applicable.
6) Score coaching quality.
7) Score hallucination, blind copying, and unsupported transfer.
8) Score uncertainty calibration.
9) Add reviewer notes without changing the model's original answer.

The score form must say "yes" to "Recognition scored before case or condition
reveal". Otherwise the completed form is invalid.

4. RECOGNITION CRITERIA

Possession (0 to 2)
0 = Wrong team, invented possession, or a materially wrong account of possession.
1 = Partly correct, vague, or appropriately uncertain where visibility is limited.
2 = Correct and supported by the sampled frames.

Temporal sequence (0 to 4)
0 = Mostly invented, materially reversed, or unrelated sequence.
1 = One isolated event is correct but the progression is substantially wrong.
2 = Main progression is partly correct, with important omissions or ordering errors.
3 = Key events are correctly ordered with only a minor omission or error.
4 = Key visible events and transitions are correctly and clearly ordered.

Tactical phase (0 to 2)
0 = Wrong or invented phase.
1 = Plausible but vague, incomplete, or only partly supported.
2 = Correct and visibly supported phase or transition.

Main event (0 to 2)
0 = Wrong, invented, or misses the critical event.
1 = Partially correct or too vague to identify the event fully.
2 = Correct identification of the main visible event.

Outcome (0 to 2)
0 = Wrong or invented outcome.
1 = Partly correct, incomplete, or appropriately cautious because the outcome is
    not fully visible.
2 = Correct outcome supported by the sampled frames.

Visible evidence (0 to 2)
0 = Evidence is absent, contradicted, or mostly unsupported by the frames.
1 = Mix of visible evidence and vague or unsupported claims.
2 = Specific evidence accurately tied to what is visible.

Critical event missed (yes or no)
Use yes when the answer omits a decisive event required to understand the sequence,
such as a goal, save, foul, turnover, offside, restart, or final possession change
that is visible in the scored frames. Explain the decision in either case.

5. ANALOGY RELEVANCE

Analogy relevance (0 to 3, or N/A where allowed)
0 = Unrelated case or advice.
1 = Surface similarity only, without a useful tactical relationship.
2 = At least one useful tactical principle transfers to Dataset B.
3 = Strongly analogous, with useful principles and important differences recognised.

This dimension evaluates the relevance of the supplied Dataset A material, not the
quality of the model's prose. Use N/A only for B0 because no Dataset A material was
provided. B5 still supplies Dataset A advice, so it must receive a relevance score.

6. COACHING CRITERIA

Problem identification (0 to 2)
0 = Unsupported, irrelevant, unsafe, or based on a misread event.
1 = Plausible but generic, secondary, or only partly supported.
2 = Identifies a priority problem that follows from Dataset B evidence.

Advice quality (0 to 3)
0 = Unsafe, irrelevant, contradictory, or unusable advice.
1 = Generic advice with little connection to the sequence.
2 = Specific and plausible advice connected to the identified problem.
3 = Specific, actionable, appropriately prioritised, and evidence-linked advice.

Practice representativeness (0 to 2)
0 = Practice is unrelated or trains the wrong problem.
1 = Partly representative but missing useful constraints or success cues.
2 = Practice reproduces the relevant problem and gives usable constraints and cues.

Coaching evidence support (0 to 2)
0 = Coaching claims do not follow from Dataset B evidence.
1 = Some claims are supported but others are generic or assumed.
2 = Problem, advice, and practice are consistently tied to visible Dataset B evidence.

7. HALLUCINATION CATEGORIES AND SEVERITY

Select one or more categories, separated by commas, or select none:

- object or relation: invents, removes, or misstates a player, ball, goal, contact,
  spatial relation, or possession relation;
- temporal: invents, reverses, or misorders an event or causal sequence;
- semantic detail: adds an unsupported fine detail, identity, intent, technique,
  body part, or exact action;
- extrinsic factual: asserts a checkable outside fact not supported by permitted
  inputs and the fact is false or unjustified;
- extrinsic non-factual: adds unsupported opinion, intention, tactical assumption,
  or speculation as if observed;
- none: no unsupported material claim.

Hallucination severity (0 to 3, lower is better)
0 = None.
1 = Minor unsupported detail that does not change the main interpretation or advice.
2 = Material unsupported claim that changes recognition, diagnosis, or advice.
3 = Unsupported claims dominate the answer or reverse the event interpretation.

Severity 0 must use category "none". A positive severity must use at least one
specific category. Explain the exact claim and why it is unsupported.

8. BLIND COPYING AND UNSUPPORTED TRANSFER

Blind copying (0 to 2, lower is better)
0 = No Dataset A-specific detail is presented as a fact about Dataset B.
1 = Wording or a detail appears partly copied without enough adaptation.
2 = Material Dataset A details are treated as observed facts about Dataset B.

Unsupported transfer (0 to 2, lower is better)
0 = Transferred principle is relevant and adjusted to Dataset B, or no transfer occurs.
1 = Transfer is questionable, generic, or insufficiently adapted.
2 = Transfer materially conflicts with Dataset B evidence or produces wrong advice.

For B0 only, both dimensions are N/A because no Dataset A material was supplied.
Use numeric scores for B1 to B5. A generally sensible football principle can still
be unsupported transfer if the current frames do not justify applying it.

9. UNCERTAINTY CALIBRATION

Uncertainty calibration (0 to 2)
0 = Confident despite missing or contradictory evidence, or so uncertain that clear
    visible evidence is not recognised.
1 = Partly calibrated but overconfident, underconfident, or incomplete in places.
2 = Confidence and stated limitations match what the sampled frames permit.

10. N/A, MISSING VALUES, AND REPORTING RULES

- N/A is permitted only for B0 analogy relevance, B0 blind copying, and B0
  unsupported transfer. Give a reason such as "B0 supplied no Dataset A material."
- Do not use N/A for recognition, coaching, hallucination severity, uncertainty,
  critical-event assessment, B1 to B5 transfer checks, or B5 analogy relevance.
- A blank score, blank required reason, out-of-range integer, invalid category, or
  invalid yes/no value makes the form invalid. Do not guess or silently impute it.
- If evidence is difficult to see, score what is supportable and reflect the
  limitation in the reason and uncertainty score. Visibility difficulty is not a
  reason to leave a required score blank.
- Keep every dimension and failure mode separate in analysis. Do not sum recognition,
  coaching, relevance, hallucination, copying, transfer, and uncertainty into one
  overall mark.
- Never edit or repair the preserved model response while scoring it.
